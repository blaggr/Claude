# Research ethics, equity, privacy & reproducibility

This system supports research on **the application of AI in child welfare and
human services workforce development**. It processes human-subjects data about
**vulnerable populations** — children, families under state scrutiny, and a
frontline workforce — and studies AI systems used on them. These are operating
requirements, not guidelines. When in doubt, stop and consult your IRB.

## 1. Vulnerable populations & IRB

- Studies involving families, case-derived data, or workers go through IRB
  review. The Design stage produces an **IRB-ready protocol summary** (purpose,
  population, instruments, risks, consent, data handling) noting the
  vulnerable-population status.
- No data collection or ingest proceeds in the loop until an **IRB approval id**
  is recorded in the run state (`ethics.require_irb_record: true`).
- The system does **not** replace IRB review. It prepares materials for it.

## 2. Algorithmic equity (central, not optional)

- Every study **pre-specifies** a subgroup / disparate-impact analysis at the
  Design gate (`equity.require_subgroup_analysis: true`): which served
  subgroups, which fairness metrics, which reference group, and the minimum cell
  size below which a subgroup is reported as inestimable.
- The Analyst runs that analysis and never silently drops or pools away a small
  subgroup. The Interpreter applies a responsible-AI lens. The Critic refuses to
  pass AI-benefit claims that ignore equity or downplay disparate impact.
- An overall benefit is never reported as an equitable benefit without evidence.

## 3. No operational use

- Outputs inform research and policy. They are **never** used to score, screen,
  or make decisions about an individual child, family, or case.
- The system does not build, train, or operate any child-welfare AI tool — it
  studies such tools. This is an explicit non-goal in `PLAN.md`.

## 4. Consent

- Instruments generated by the Methodologist include consent language.
- Data use is scoped to the approved research question; reusing data for a new
  question is a new cycle with its own review.

## 5. De-identification & PII

- Data enters the loop **only after PII and case identifiers are removed**. With
  `ethics.require_deidentified: true`, ingest is refused unless the dataset is
  flagged de-identified.
- `data/` is **git-ignored by default** — never commit raw, identifiable, or
  case-level child-welfare data.
- PII and case identifiers must never be placed in prompts sent to the LLM. Pass
  aggregates, codes, or de-identified rows only.

## 6. Reproducibility

- Every report ships with a **methods appendix** and an **audit trail**: which
  agent, which prompt version, which inputs produced each claim.
- Prompts are versioned; the version is recorded per stage in run state.
- Outputs are run-stamped so a report can always be traced to its inputs.

## 7. AI disclosure & accountability

- Reports disclose that AI agents assisted and that human review gates were
  applied at each stage — fitting for a program that studies responsible AI use.
- Agents **propose**; humans **decide**. Every stage has a human gate; the final
  report requires explicit human approval before release.
- The Critic's adversarial + equity review and any unresolved flags are retained
  in the audit trail, not silently dropped.

## 8. Data retention

- Follow your institution's and agency's retention and destruction schedule for
  the dataset.
- Run state and outputs may contain quotes/aggregates derived from data — treat
  them with the same care as the source data.
