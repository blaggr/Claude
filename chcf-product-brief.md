# Product Brief: The Safety-Net AI Workforce Readiness & Impact Index

**Prepared for:** Rob Blagg (AVAL UCLA)
**Target funder:** California Health Care Foundation (CHCF)
**Date:** July 18, 2026

## The one-line answer

Build the **measurement backbone for CHCF's "AI-Powered Workforce" priority**: a standardized evaluation toolkit plus an annual public indicators report that tracks AI training, adoption, and impact across California's safety-net workforce — a product CHCF has explicitly said it lacks, that no incumbent grantee owns, and that maps one-to-one onto AVAL's existing training-measurement and workforce-indicators infrastructure.

## Why this, why now

CHCF grants roughly $42.5M per year (2024 Form 990) and in 2026 reorganized around four named priorities: The New Uninsured, The Future of Medi-Cal, Health Care Affordability, and **An AI-Powered Workforce** — "putting emerging technology to work for safety net providers and their patients."

Within that fourth priority, CHCF has publicly named two persistent, unsolved barriers:

1. **"Establishing systematic ways to evaluate the performance and impact of AI tools"** in safety-net settings.
2. **"Equipping frontline staff with foundational AI knowledge and skills."**

Those two barriers are, respectively, *program evaluation* and *training measurement* — the two things AVAL already does at production scale for human-services agencies. CHCF is actively piloting AI tools in safety-net clinics but has no standardized instrument set, no cross-site comparability, and no recurring public scorecard for whether the safety-net workforce is actually ready for or benefiting from these tools.

Meanwhile, projected allocations are being pulled toward Medi-Cal defense (H.R. 1 cuts ~$30B/year in federal funding from Medi-Cal), which *raises* the value of the AI-workforce bet: CHCF's own framing is that AI must stretch a shrinking safety-net workforce further. Every dollar they move into AI pilots increases their need to prove impact.

## Why you uniquely

- **Turnkey training measurement.** The AWE pipeline (objective-mapped assessments, Qualtrics QSF automation, Kirkpatrick-classified dashboards, eLearning modules) is exactly the machinery needed to standardize "foundational AI knowledge and skills" measurement across CHW/P/R, clinic staff, and behavioral health workers.
- **Workforce indicators reporting.** The California Workforce Indicators report format is a proven, publication-grade vehicle — CHCF is a publish-heavy funder (Health Policy Survey, explainers, briefs) and buys exactly this genre.
- **Mixed methods at scale.** AI-assisted qualitative coding with inter-coder reliability lets the Index capture frontline experience (trust, workflow fit, equity concerns) alongside quantitative adoption metrics — the equity lens CHCF requires in all work.
- **Positioning.** UCSF Healthforce Center owns clinician supply and leadership development for CHCF. Nobody owns AI workforce readiness measurement. A UCLA-based offering complements rather than collides with the incumbent.

## The product

1. **A shared instrument set** (pre/post AI training assessments, adoption and trust surveys, Qualtrics-ready) offered to every CHCF AI pilot site — creating cross-site comparability CHCF cannot get today.
2. **Per-pilot Kirkpatrick dashboards** (reaction → learning → behavior/adoption → results) delivered to grantees and CHCF program officers.
3. **An annual "Safety-Net AI Workforce Readiness & Impact" indicators report** published with CHCF — the recurring, citable artifact that becomes the field's reference point.

## Sizing and entry

CHCF grants typically run $100K–$1M with multi-year investments for statewide initiatives. Entry paths: a direct concept memo to the AI portfolio team; the CHCF Innovation Fund; or watching for the next Medi-Cal "Bold Ideas" style call (the December 2025 round paid $50K per developed concept). The fastest wedge is a pilot: instrument one existing CHCF AI pilot cohort and produce the first dashboard within a quarter.
